INTERVIEW GUIDANCE

Interview preparation should be based on the selected job advertisement, including its responsibilities, requirements, skills, qualifications, technologies, and experience requirements.

The assistant can help candidates prepare for interviews by:

Explaining the responsibilities of the selected role.

Identifying skills and qualifications mentioned in the job advertisement.

Identifying technologies, tools, and knowledge areas mentioned in the job advertisement.

Suggesting relevant behavioural interview questions based on the selected role.

Suggesting technical interview preparation where appropriate.

Helping the candidate structure answers using the STAR method.

Helping the candidate identify relevant examples from their actual experience.

Helping the candidate explain university projects, personal projects, internships, coursework, or other relevant experience.

Helping the candidate prepare questions to ask the interviewer.

STAR METHOD

The STAR method can be used to structure answers to behavioural interview questions.

Situation:
Describe the relevant situation or context. Explain where and when the problem occurred.

Task:
Explain the responsibility, objective, or problem that needed to be addressed.

Action:
Explain the specific actions taken by the candidate. Focus on the candidate's own contribution, decisions, problem-solving approach, and collaboration.

Result:
Explain the outcome of the actions. Where possible, describe the result using a measurable outcome. If there was no measurable result, explain what was learned or how the experience improved the candidate's approach.

PROBLEM-SOLVING QUESTIONS

For a behavioural question about problem-solving, a useful STAR answer should explain:

What problem or challenge occurred.

Why the problem needed to be solved.

What the candidate was responsible for.

How the candidate investigated or understood the problem.

What options or solutions were considered.

What actions the candidate personally took.

How the candidate worked with others where relevant.

What the result was.

What the candidate learned from the experience.

Example question:

"Tell me about a time you solved a difficult problem."

The assistant can guide the candidate to structure the answer as:

Situation:
Describe the project, task, or situation where the problem occurred.

Task:
Explain what needed to be achieved and what responsibility the candidate had.

Action:
Explain how the candidate investigated the problem, identified possible solutions, selected an approach, implemented it, and worked with others if necessary.

Result:
Explain the outcome and any lessons learned.

ROLE RELEVANCE

Interview preparation should be connected to the selected job.

If the job advertisement explicitly mentions a skill, qualification, technology, responsibility, or type of experience, the assistant can use that information when suggesting interview preparation.

For example, if the selected job requires problem-solving, teamwork, software development, or knowledge of a particular technology, interview preparation can include questions and examples related to those areas.

The assistant should not claim that a skill, technology, qualification, responsibility, interview stage, company process, or employer expectation exists unless it is provided by the selected job advertisement or another reliable source.

CANDIDATE EXPERIENCE

The assistant must not assume that the candidate has experience with a technology, qualification, project, internship, employment situation, or professional responsibility.

Interview answers should be based on the candidate's actual experience.

For graduate and entry-level roles, potentially relevant examples may include:

University projects.

Coursework.

Personal software projects.

Internships.

Part-time employment.

Group assignments.

Hackathons.

Volunteer activities.

Other genuine experiences that demonstrate relevant skills.

The assistant should clearly distinguish between:

Information explicitly stated in the job advertisement.

Information provided by the candidate.

General interview preparation advice.

Information retrieved from organisational knowledge sources.

RAG USAGE

When relevant organisational knowledge is retrieved, the assistant should use the retrieved information to improve the response.

The assistant should not present retrieved knowledge as candidate-specific experience.

The assistant should use retrieved knowledge to provide relevant guidance, such as:

Explaining how STAR should be structured.

Identifying what a strong behavioural answer should contain.

Connecting interview preparation to the selected job.

Suggesting areas the candidate should prepare.

The frontend may display the retrieved knowledge sources so the user can see where the interview guidance came from.

SOURCE TRANSPARENCY

When the AI response uses retrieved knowledge, the response may include the names of the retrieved knowledge sources.

Example:

Sources:

interview_guidance.txt

career_guidance.txt

The source names should be displayed as supporting information rather than as part of the AI's main answer.

RESPONSE QUALITY

AI responses should be:

Relevant to the selected job.

Based on retrieved information when applicable.

Clear and concise.

Practical for interview preparation.

Explicit about assumptions.

Based on the candidate's actual experience when discussing personal examples.

The assistant should avoid:

Inventing candidate experience.

Inventing company interview processes.

Inventing interview stages.

Inventing employer expectations.

Providing generic STAR explanations when the selected job provides relevant information that can be used instead.

The goal of interview guidance is to help the candidate prepare a genuine, job-relevant answer rather than generate fictional experience.
