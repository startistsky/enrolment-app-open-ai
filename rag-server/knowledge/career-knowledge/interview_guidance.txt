INTERVIEW GUIDANCE

Interview preparation should be based on the responsibilities, requirements, skills, qualifications, and experience described in the selected job advertisement.

General interview preparation activities include:

- Understand the organisation and the role.
- Review the responsibilities listed in the job description.
- Review the required and preferred skills.
- Review required qualifications.
- Identify technologies, tools, or knowledge areas mentioned in the job advertisement.
- Prepare examples of relevant experience, coursework, projects, internships, or other activities.
- Prepare explanations of relevant projects and clearly describe the candidate's own contribution.
- Practise behavioural interview questions.
- Practise technical interview questions where appropriate.
- Practise explaining technical concepts clearly.
- Prepare questions to ask the interviewer.

The STAR approach can be used for behavioural interview questions:

Situation:
Describe the relevant situation or context.

Task:
Explain the responsibility, objective, or problem.

Action:
Explain the specific actions taken.

Result:
Explain the outcome or what was learned.

Interview questions should be relevant to the selected job.

If a job advertisement requests a particular skill, qualification, technology, responsibility, or type of experience, interview preparation can focus on that requirement.

Candidates should prepare answers based on their actual experience. The assistant should not assume that a candidate has experience with a technology, qualification, responsibility, project, or professional situation unless the candidate provides that information.

For graduate and entry-level roles, relevant coursework, university projects, personal projects, internships, and transferable skills may provide useful examples when they are genuinely part of the candidate's experience.

Candidates should distinguish between:
- What the job advertisement explicitly requires.
- What the candidate has actually done.
- General preparation advice.

The assistant should not invent interview questions, company interview processes, interview stages, or employer expectations that are not provided by the job advertisement or another reliable source.
