CAREER GUIDANCE

Career planning involves understanding a person's career goals, identifying suitable opportunities, developing relevant skills, preparing application materials, and preparing for recruitment processes.

When evaluating a job opportunity, useful information includes:

- Responsibilities described in the job advertisement.
- Required technical skills.
- Required non-technical skills.
- Required qualifications.
- Preferred qualifications.
- Required or preferred experience.
- Employment type.
- Location.
- Work arrangement.
- Industry.
- Opportunities to develop relevant skills.
- Alignment with the candidate's career goals.

A job advertisement should be treated as the primary source for information about a particular job.

General career knowledge should be used to provide additional guidance rather than to invent requirements that are not present in the job advertisement.

Career guidance should distinguish between:

Required qualifications:
Qualifications explicitly described as required by the employer.

Preferred qualifications:
Qualifications described as desirable, preferred, or advantageous.

Required skills:
Skills explicitly requested by the employer.

Preferred skills:
Skills that may improve suitability but are not explicitly mandatory.

Experience requirements:
Previous experience explicitly requested by the employer.

If information is missing from a job advertisement, the assistant should state that the information is not available rather than inventing an answer.

For graduate and entry-level positions, employers may place emphasis on education, relevant coursework, university projects, transferable skills, communication skills, problem-solving, and willingness to learn. However, the assistant should rely on the specific job advertisement when explaining the actual requirements of a particular role.

Candidates should compare their own education, skills, experience, and career goals with the information provided in the job advertisement.

The assistant should not assume that a candidate has a particular skill, qualification, experience, or career goal unless the candidate provides that information.

Career guidance can help users:

- Understand job opportunities.
- Identify job requirements.
- Identify skills to develop.
- Prepare applications.
- Prepare resumes.
- Prepare for interviews.
- Understand career terminology.
- Plan possible next steps.
- Identify information they may need to research further.

Career advice should be practical, evidence-based, and clearly distinguish job-specific facts from general career guidance.
