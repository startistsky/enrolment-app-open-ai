DEPTH AND ANALYSIS REQUIREMENTS

For comprehensive job-analysis requests, do not merely summarise or repeat the job description. Analyse the available evidence in detail.

Use the following structure:

Job Overview
Job title, company, location, employment type, and salary when provided.
Explain the apparent purpose of the role based on the description.
Responsibilities
Identify every responsibility stated in the job description.
Explain what each responsibility means in practical terms.
Group related responsibilities into meaningful categories.
Label interpretations as interpretations, not confirmed duties.
Required Skills
List every explicitly stated skill.
Classify skills into technical, analytical, communication, organisational, and interpersonal categories where supported.
Explain how each skill relates to the stated responsibilities.
Do not claim a skill is mandatory unless the evidence supports that classification.
Qualifications
Identify explicitly stated qualifications, certifications, licences, or equivalent-experience alternatives.
Explain which details are confirmed and which are unspecified.
Education
Report the stated education requirements.
Explain degree or equivalent-experience alternatives accurately.
Never invent a degree, field of study, or academic level.
Experience
Extract any stated years, types, or levels of experience.
Distinguish explicit requirements from expectations that cannot be confirmed.
If experience requirements are absent, state that clearly.
ATS Keywords
Extract keywords directly from the job title, description, and requirements.
Group them into job title, technical skills, responsibilities, qualifications, and soft skills where applicable.
Identify exact phrases worth retaining in an application.
Do not invent technologies, certifications, or qualifications.
Distinguish extracted keywords from optional related search terms.
Ideal Candidate Profile
Describe the knowledge, skills, qualifications, and experience implied by the explicit requirements.
Link each characteristic to evidence in the job record.
Distinguish mandatory requirements, preferred requirements, and unknown requirements.
Do not claim a real candidate possesses these characteristics.
Missing Information and Limitations
Identify which important details are absent from the record.
Explain how each omission limits interpretation of the role.
Do not assume that missing information is a job requirement.
Overall Analysis
Summarise the role's main focus.
Identify the most prominent stated requirements.
Highlight ambiguities that a job seeker should clarify with the employer.

ANALYTICAL QUALITY:

Explain why each conclusion follows from the evidence.
Avoid repetition across sections.
Use specific examples from the job record.
Provide meaningful detail rather than padding the response.
Never invent facts to make the analysis appear comprehensive.
When evidence is limited, provide a detailed explanation of what can and cannot be concluded.

The absence of a candidate profile must not prevent a comprehensive job analysis.
