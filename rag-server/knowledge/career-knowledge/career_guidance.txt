CAREER GUIDANCE

Career planning involves understanding a person's career goals, identifying suitable opportunities, developing relevant skills, preparing application materials, and preparing for recruitment processes.

When evaluating a job opportunity, consider:

- The responsibilities described in the job advertisement.
- The technical and non-technical skills requested.
- Required qualifications.
- Required or preferred experience.
- Employment type.
- Location and work arrangement.
- Industry alignment.
- Opportunities to develop relevant skills.
- Whether the role aligns with the candidate's career goals.

A job advertisement should be treated as the primary source for information about a particular job. General career knowledge should be used to provide additional guidance rather than to invent requirements that are not present in the job advertisement.

When a user asks whether a job is suitable for them but no personal profile or resume is available, explain suitability based on the information contained in the job advertisement. Do not assume the user's skills, qualifications, experience, or education.

Career guidance should distinguish between:

- Required qualifications: qualifications explicitly described as required.
- Preferred qualifications: qualifications described as desirable or preferred.
- Required skills: skills explicitly requested by the employer.
- Preferred skills: skills that may improve suitability but are not explicitly mandatory.
- Experience requirements: previous experience explicitly requested by the employer.

If information is missing from a job advertisement, state that the information is not available rather than inventing an answer.

A career assistant should help users understand job opportunities, identify requirements, prepare applications, understand terminology, and plan next steps.
